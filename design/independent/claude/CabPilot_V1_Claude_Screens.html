1 · Navigation structure

Four destinations and one capture button. Today is where the app opens and where a driver spends the working day. The centre Scan button is the single way to get paper into CabPilot; it always asks, or proves, which load the paper belongs to.

2 · First run

Three screens, no password (TM-D051). The only thing asked beyond the phone number is a backup email, with a plain warning about what happens without one, and how the driver is paid, which picks the tier.

3 · Today & the Current Load Card

The card is built top-down in the order a driver needs things at the gate: which stop → the number → where & when → what must not go wrong → gate details → paperwork. The next step is always the big green button in the thumb zone, never inside the scrolling card.

4 · Scan, import & attribution

All paper comes through the centre button. Every scan answers two questions before it's saved: what is it and which load is it for. When CabPilot is sure, the answer is pre-selected with the reason shown. When it isn't, nothing is pre-selected and the driver must pick.

12 · Offline, sync & notifications

Offline is a normal state, not an error. One pill in the header says it ("Offline · 3 saved"). Items waiting to sync carry an "on phone" tag, and only a real conflict interrupts the driver.

13 · Lean admin panel (web)

A desktop web tool with five areas and three roles. The privacy boundary is visible in the interface: staff see that documents exist and when and where they were uploaded, never what is in them.

14 · Reasoning behind the major decisions

One card, one button, fixed position

The next step (Check In → Check Out → Scan BOL → Check In → Scan POD) is always the full-width green button above the tab bar, in the same spot. A driver builds muscle memory once. The card scrolls; the button never does.

The number is the hero

At a guard shack the question is "what's your pickup number?" So the card leads with it at 34px in a typeface built for low vision (Atkinson Hyperlegible: distinct 0/O, 1/l/I), one tap to copy. After pickup the hero switches to BOL / delivery ref.

Gate info lives on the card

TM-D049 rules out a Gate Pass screen, so the gate details (carrier, DOT/MC, truck, trailer, plates, driver) sit in a compact grid on the pickup card. Zero taps at the gate. Anything more is left to field testing, as the decision says.

Amber means "don't get this wrong"

Only Critical Load Instructions use solid amber. Countdown chips use light amber and red. Green is reserved for actions and "done". Colour is never the only signal: every chip also carries words.

Scan is the one way in

A single centre button takes every kind of paper. Classification and load attribution happen after capture, with the reason shown ("matches PU# 4471-0923"). The driver never has to decide where to go before scanning.

Automation announces itself

Stage changes from documents appear as a toast with Undo, never silently. Check In has no confirm dialog; Undo replaces "Are you sure?". That saves a tap on every correct action and still recovers mistakes.

Driving view instead of blocking

Rather than greying out the app while moving, Today switches to large read-only facts and one navigation button. The driver still sees what they need and there is nothing to type.

Four tabs, not five

Today, Loads, Wallet, More, plus Scan. Money and Upcoming are important but are desk tasks, so they go under More. Upcoming's top 3 still show on Today, satisfying "near the top of the working experience".

Provenance as small tags, not warnings

Every time and number carries a quiet tag (GPS · Edited · on phone · read from page). The record is honest and auditable without making the driver read disclaimers.

Offline is a state, not an error

One header pill plus "on phone" tags. Only a real manual-vs-manual conflict interrupts the driver. Everything else syncs quietly and idempotently.

Each is a spot where the approved requirements, turned into screens, leave a gap, a tension or an ambiguity. The design above shows the least-assuming behaviour, marked "assumed", pending owner decision.
